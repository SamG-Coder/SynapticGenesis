# Completed comparison of three model sizes

All three randomly initialized native C++/CUDA models completed the same
four-stage prose curriculum. The largest has 104,851,472 parameters and 32,768
spiking neurons, versus 1,951,624 parameters and 2,048 neurons in the small
control. More capacity did not improve the final development scores under
this common learning policy: both larger models finish behind the small one.

The [complete result](../reports/prose-size-comparison.json) preserves all
twelve assessments, 72 book evaluations and 48 raw continuations. Its
[publication audit](../reports/prose-size-comparison-audit.json) verifies every
native assessment command, output, checkpoint and exposure counter. The
[declared protocol](prose-evaluation.md) explains selection and timing boundaries.

## Identical learning exposure

The selected training collection contains 55 books, 26,896,416 source bytes
and 4,817,047 word occurrences. Each model reaches 216,289 source observations,
27,680,129 source next-byte targets, 54,072 replay updates and 6,918,179 replayed
targets. There are 270,361 optimizer updates and 41,472 generated bytes;
generated text is not used as a training target. The initial reading stage
receives four passes, which accounts for source exposure exceeding the source
collection size. Word occurrences are not distinct vocabulary entries.

Source, replay and scheduled-generation counters match at all four boundaries.
The complete replay policy payload, including stored examples and sampling
state, is byte-identical across sizes at each boundary. Seed 1337, learning
rate 0.0003, 128-byte chunks, source order and replay policy are shared.
Learning uses the declared fast math path; assessment uses strict FP32.

This is one seed and one common learning rate. It does not establish the best
achievable quality of each size or an inherent advantage of small networks.
The extra width and depth can require a different learning configuration.

## Prediction and retention

The following is the equal-book mean over the four validation books, in nats
per target byte; lower is better. Each book contributes the same fixed 65,536
sampled target bytes, assessed through independent 128-byte windows.

| Profile | Stage 1: 8,176 observations | Stage 2: 26,110 | Stage 3: 95,207 | Stage 4: 216,289 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2M | 2.200162 | 1.911111 | 1.769407 | 1.800033 |
| 27M | 2.181080 | 1.824148 | 1.810412 | 2.281865 |
| 105M | 2.192909 | 1.866187 | 1.804779 | 1.967449 |

The larger models outperform the small control at stage two, but the ordering
reverses by stage three. Stage four worsens the validation mean by 0.030626,
0.471452 and 0.162670 nats/byte for 2M, 27M and 105M respectively. Both larger
models worsen on all six individual book checks during that last stage.
The 27M final mean is also worse than its first-stage mean.

| Final book loss | 2M | 27M | 105M |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| New National First Reader (validation) | 2.202022 | 2.753289 | 2.473894 |
| Home Geography for Primary Grades (validation) | 1.761930 | 2.282519 | 1.927355 |
| The Velveteen Rabbit (validation) | 1.606762 | 2.038158 | 1.720305 |
| Far from the Madding Crowd (validation) | 1.629416 | 2.053492 | 1.748244 |
| McGuffey's Eclectic Primer (training monitor) | 1.781404 | 2.156668 | 1.932688 |
| McGuffey's First Eclectic Reader (training monitor) | 1.719730 | 2.002063 | 1.906738 |

Relative to stage one, the final training-monitor losses change as follows.
Positive values indicate worse prediction on the same sampled earlier text.

| Profile | Primer change | First Reader change |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2M | +0.022022 | -0.023242 |
| 27M | +0.526852 | +0.372122 |
| 105M | +0.340107 | +0.306184 |

These training monitors measure retention of next-byte prediction on old
material, not a count of remembered facts. The validation scores measure
short-window prediction; no reserved test book was scored.

## Speed and next controlled experiments

The separate [bounded live-capacity benchmark](bounded-live-memory.md) measured
the following warm medians on the RTX 5080. Tokens here are bytes, not subword
tokens. Learning throughput includes replay and scheduled speech, but
excludes initialization, warmup and checkpoint saves. Generation measures
1,024 bytes in each of three warm rounds.

| Profile | Parameters | Spiking neurons | Learning source bytes/s | Generation bytes/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2M | 1,951,624 | 2,048 | 35,725 | 6,244 |
| 27M | 27,260,432 | 16,384 | 12,427 | 2,743 |
| 105M | 104,851,472 | 32,768 | 7,056 | 1,348 |

Those timings come from the controlled capacity experiment, not this full
curriculum comparison. The original 105M continuation saved every 8,192
observations while the smaller runs saved every 2,048, so total curriculum
session times are not a fair speed ranking. The [105M completion record](prose-105m-complete.md)
separately reports its measured 62.5 minutes of learning sessions including saves.

The [completed neuron-response panel](prose-spike-results.md) finds that larger
networks fire more densely and have more zero local spike derivatives. Separate
declared studies on the `research/learning-rate-retention` and
`research/replay-capacity` branches test a smaller final-stage learning rate
and a larger stored replay sample. Their outcomes must be measured before
claiming a repair or promoting a model for reproduction. The prepared astronomy
extension remains unused by these three founders.

To reproduce publication verification without training again, use fresh paths:

```powershell
python scripts/publish_prose_sizes.py --report runs/prose-size-recheck.json --audit runs/prose-size-recheck-audit.json
```

The publisher checks recorded artifacts and native execution journals; it is
not an additional numerical oracle, new seed or fresh speed measurement.
