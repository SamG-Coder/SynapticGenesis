# Early width and core learning-rate results

All fifteen fresh models in the [declared study](prose-early-width.md) completed.
Reducing the core rate reduces firing and the fraction of locally zero spike
surrogates, but the language benefit is inconsistent across seeds. The 27M
control has the lowest mean validation loss in this early-stage screen. These
results do not establish a default rate for the larger 411M model.

The [published results](../reports/prose-early-width-results.json) contain every
book score, all sixty raw generations and all ninety layer-cache observations.
The [publication audit](../reports/prose-early-width-publication.json) verifies
the two guard calls, fifteen learners, 150 assessments and 295 raw-coordinate
files. No production model equations changed for this study.

## Matched learning and validation

Each model learns the first four selected readers for four passes: 8,176 source
observations and 1,045,096 next-byte targets. All models have eight layers and
four spiking neurons per channel per layer. Seeds are 1337, 2027 and 4099.
Replay capacity is 1,024, with one replay update every four source updates;
generation runs every 500 source updates through the production live loop.
The output normalization and vocabulary head retain learning rate 0.0003.
Core multipliers apply to the embedding and spiking blocks from initialization.

The table reports the equal-weight mean across the four validation books,
in nats per byte. Lower is better. Standard deviations describe these three
seeds, not a confidence interval. Two separate training-book monitors are
retained in the report; reserved test books were not scored.

Halving the 27M core rate improves one seed and worsens two; its mean difference
is +0.002255 nats/byte. Quartering the 105M core rate improves two seeds and
worsens one; its mean difference is -0.010792. Neither result is a consistent
per-seed improvement. Every source update in all fifteen learners triggers the
existing global gradient clip; the observer does not count replay clipping.

## Activity changes and their limits

The first source chunk has similar firing across all five cases, approximately
42.5-43.7%. Later observations separate strongly. The following ranges are across
the three seeds after averaging the eight layers equally:

| Case | Firing at source update 2,048 | Firing at update 8,176 | Zero local surrogate at 8,176 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7M control | 41.40-41.93% | 44.45-44.70% | 22.73-22.98% |
| 27M control | 56.04-56.84% | 67.19-69.39% | 52.98-54.57% |
| 105M control | 85.20-85.97% | 93.76-94.38% | 87.80-88.92% |
| 27M half core rate | 40.83-42.26% | 45.36-47.15% | 23.50-24.72% |
| 105M quarter core rate | 39.22-41.01% | 43.48-46.00% | 20.79-23.11% |

Update 2,048 covers 128 targets. Update 8,176 is the final **ten-byte document
tail**, so its values are a local observation, not whole-corpus activity.
The caches precede that source update and include the live recurrent history
and scheduled speech. A zero local spike surrogate does not establish that
every gradient path is zero or that a neuron is permanently inactive.

The reduced rates clearly affect activity in these runs. Lower firing by itself
does not prove better language, faster dense CUDA execution or lower energy
use. The study does not implement biological development or test an adaptive
homeostatic controller. Later-source retention remains untested for these
rate candidates.

## Output and integrity

The raw generations still show spelling errors and weak sentence structure.
For the fixed first prompt `The bird `, the seed-1337 105M control begins:

```text
The bird moning in
a now hi live son find every lingir to night it.
```

The corresponding quarter-rate model begins:

```text
The bird sarts
of and soon quick and where.
```

These are first-line excerpts from the first predetermined sample, not answers
selected for quality. The report preserves all 256 generated bytes for every
sample, including output hex. No useful question-answering result is claimed.

Both tiny guard checkpoints and their scheduled speech match production
exactly. The seed-1337 27M and 105M controls also reproduce their preserved
initial/final checkpoints, six book scores and four samples exactly. Same-width
rate pairs start with identical neural/optimizer/recurrent arrays; their
declared core-rate field differs. All cases for each seed have matched source,
replay and speech policy/state at the endpoint.

The publication independently reconstructs sampled coordinate statistics and
source/update boundaries; it does not independently recompute neural forward
activity. The original failed speech-framing audit remains preserved and is
explained in the [correction record](early-width-speech-audit.md). No study case
ran in that failed attempt.

Integration compiles both optional diagnostics and passes their host checks,
the seven build-selector checks, all 21 conflicting-selector pairs, and the
publication's six altered-aggregate checks. The [integration report](../reports/early-width-integration.json)
records the exact binaries, sources and preserved study inputs. These integration
checks execute no GPU model work and do not rerun or replace the completed
experiment. Instrumented times are not reported as production throughput.

No model, learning rate or source is promoted for teaching or reproduction.
The larger saved study keeps its declared policy while it tests a different
capacity over all four source stages.
