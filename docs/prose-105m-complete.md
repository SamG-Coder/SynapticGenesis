# Complete prose exposure of the 105M founder

The randomly initialized 104,851,472-parameter, 32,768-neuron model completed
all four selected prose stages. The final native process exited successfully;
its session, immutable stage-four checkpoint and latest checkpoint agree on
the declared source, replay and scheduled-generation counts. The
[complete report](../reports/prose-105m-complete.json) preserves these records,
all four fixed assessments and all sixteen raw 256-byte continuations.

The outcome is mixed: validation prediction improved through stage three,
then worsened on every validation book during stage four. Prediction on both
early training readers also worsened. The final question-shaped prompts
produce story fragments rather than correct answers. More capacity and a
complete pass through the larger collection have not yet produced useful QA.

The first session took 156.04 seconds and the continuation took 3,594.85
seconds. Their sum is about 62.5 minutes. These measurements include replay,
scheduled speech and intermediate/final saves; they exclude initialization,
the initial checkpoint save and the gap between invocations. The continuation
alone reports 7,409.21 source bytes/s. These are byte tokens, not word pieces.

The continuation's update-tick p95 is 28.22 ms and its update-plus-96-byte-speech
tick p95 is 108.08 ms. Both are histogram-bin upper bounds with relative bin
width about 2.19%. These do not measure request-serving latency for arbitrary
prompts. The separate [capacity benchmark](bounded-live-memory.md) remains the
controlled throughput comparison; the pending smaller founders save more often.

The final mean remains below the first-stage mean, largely alongside the
improvement on the held-out novel. It is 0.162670 nats/byte worse than stage
three. The early training readers finish 0.340107 and 0.306184 nats/byte worse
than stage one. Those are regressions in prediction on the same sampled old
material, not an estimate of how many facts the model forgot.

All forty native assessment commands completed. Each saved checkpoint was
unchanged after its six book assessments and four generations. The first-stage
reader result also matches the earlier exploratory assessment. The complete
three-size orchestration is still running, so these results establish no
quality advantage over the matched 2M or 27M models.

These examples fail to answer the questions. They are qualitative probes,
not a statistically meaningful QA accuracy benchmark. The source curriculum
is mainly book pretraining, and question formatting alone does not supply
instruction training.

The declared smaller-model comparison and matched neuron-response panel
continue unchanged. Their results should separate effects of capacity from
response saturation and the shared learning policy. The prepared astronomy
extension remains unused. Before increasing exposure again, investigate the
measured earlier-material regression with matched source and replay budgets;
do not treat a later checkpoint, more neurons or chronological age as evidence
of a better learner. No checkpoint is promoted for reproduction by this report.
